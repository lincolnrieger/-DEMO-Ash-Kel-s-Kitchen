Camp menus

Two ways to feed a camp. We cook the lot, or we send in the provisions and your group takes it from there. Either way the shopping, the planning and the dietary list are ours to worry about.

Option one

We cook, you eat

Everything is cooked on site in the camp kitchen. No depot food, no reheated trays off a truck. You give us your arrival and departure times and we build the meal times around your program.

Every day covers

  • Breakfast — cereals, toast, a hot option, tea and coffee
  • Morning tea — something out of the oven, and fruit
  • Lunch — hot or cold, packed to take out if the day runs long
  • Afternoon tea — enough to get them through to dinner
  • Dinner and dessert — a proper hot meal, always with pudding
  • Fruit, all day — bowls out for the kids to help themselves
Download the menu

Good to know

  • The kitchen is a nut-free zone.
  • Most allergies and dietary requirements are catered for. Complex needs want a bit more notice.
  • Meal times move with your program, not the other way round.
  • Menus change with the seasons and with your group.
Option two

We provision, you cook

For smaller groups with their own hands and a working barbecue. We do the shopping and the prep. Eggs cracked and whisked, pancake mix made, lasagne assembled, crumble ready for the oven. Any delicious leftovers, pack up and take with you. Too easy.

What arrives

  • Breakfast — bread, cereals, butter, spreads, juice, milk, tea, coffee and Milo. Bacon and scrambled eggs. Pancakes with fresh whipped cream
  • Snacks — biscuits, fruit, cheeses, crackers, dip, chips, muesli bars and tubs of yoghurt
  • Lunch — hamburgers with all the trimmings. Ham and salad wraps
  • Dinner — house-made lasagne with salad and ice cream sundaes. A barbecue pack with bread, salad, house-made apple crumble and custard
  • The bits — cutlery and crockery, sauces, salt and pepper, serviettes, cling wrap, foil and oil

Your part

  1. Cook the barbecue meat
  2. Put the salads together
  3. Fry the bacon and eggs
  4. Fry the pancake mix
  5. Put the wraps together
  6. Cook the hamburger patties
  7. Heat the lasagne
  8. Heat the apple crumble
  9. Enjoy

The cook and the chef

Ashley and Kelly Parr. A husband and wife team who between them can run a commercial kitchen and cook like it is home.

A partnership built on food

Ash and Kel lead the resident catering team at Woodhouse Adventure Park, feeding school camps, corporate groups, charities and community groups across the Adelaide Hills.

Between them they bring the artistry of a commercially trained chef and the natural flair of a great home style cook. In camp catering that combination matters more than it sounds. You need someone who can plate up properly, and someone who knows what a hundred tired eleven-year-olds will actually eat.

They use the freshest ingredients they can get their hands on and source local Adelaide Hills produce wherever possible. Guests keep coming back, which in this business is the only review that counts.

“Together we are able to combine the artistry of a commercially trained chef with the natural flair of a great home style cook. And we have lots of fun doing it.”

Ash and Kel

Ashley Parr

Chef

Ashley started as an apprentice chef at 15 and trained at TAFE SA. He worked at the Three Brothers Arms, The Magic Flute and the Lenzerheide Restaurant before becoming Head Chef of Dixon's Restaurant. He also makes the stove-top espresso the teachers write home about.

Kelly Parr

Cook

Kelly has ten years in hospitality, including five as assistant restaurant manager at the Goodwood Park Hotel. She auditioned and was shortlisted for MasterChef in 2009, and has a deep repertoire of dishes, scaled from a small retreat group up to a full camp.

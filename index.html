A nut-free kitchen

The kitchen is a nut-free zone, and we cater for most allergy and dietary requirements — gluten free, dairy free, vegetarian and vegan included. Send us the list with your numbers and the more complex needs get sorted before you arrive.

The fussy ones too

Every camp has a few kids who won't touch a salad. We know who they are by the second meal, and nobody goes back to their bunk hungry. Teachers get the good coffee and a generous hand with the biscuit tin.

Cooked on site

Everything is made in the camp kitchen, not trucked in from a depot. The lasagne is homemade, the crumble is homemade, and the biscuits come out of the oven that morning.

Camp Menus

Food kids will actually eat.

Two ways to feed a camp: we cook the lot, or we send in the provisions and your group takes it from there. Either way the shopping, the planning and the dietary list are ours to worry about.

Option one — fully catered

The whole camp, handled

Everything cooked on site in the camp kitchen — no depot food, no reheated trays off a truck. You tell us the arrival and departure times and we build the meal times around your program, not the other way round.

Option two — small groups

We provision, you cook

Provisions, delivered

For smaller groups with their own hands and a working BBQ. We do the shopping and the prep — eggs cracked and whisked, pancake mix made, lasagne assembled, crumble ready for the oven — and drop it in. Any delicious leftovers, pack up and take with you. Too easy.

Typically covers
  • Breakfast — bread, cereals, butter, spreads, juice, milk, tea, coffee and Milo; bacon and scrambled eggs; pancakes with fresh whipped cream
  • Snacks — biscuits, fruit, cheeses, crackers, dip, chips, muesli bars and tubs of yoghurt
  • Lunch — hamburgers with all the trimmings; ham and salad wraps
  • Dinner — house-made lasagne with salad and ice cream sundaes; a BBQ pack with bread, salad, house-made apple crumble and custard
Your part
  • Cook the BBQ meat
  • Put the salads together
  • Fry the bacon and eggs
  • Fry the pancake mix
  • Put the wraps together
  • Cook the hamburger patties
  • Heat the lasagne
  • Heat the apple crumble
  • Enjoy
Also included

Cutlery and crockery, sauces, salt and pepper, serviettes, cling wrap, foil and oil. You won't need to bring a thing.

Our Story

The Cook & The Chef.

Ashley and Kelly Parr — a husband and wife team who between them can run a commercial kitchen and cook like it's home.

A partnership built on food.

Ash and Kel lead the resident catering team at Woodhouse Adventure Park, feeding school camps, corporate groups, charities and community groups across the Adelaide Hills.

Between them they bring the artistry of a commercially trained chef and the natural flair of a great home-style cook. In camp catering that combination matters more than it sounds: you need someone who can plate up properly and someone who knows what a hundred tired eleven-year-olds will actually eat.

They use the freshest ingredients they can get their hands on and source local Adelaide Hills produce wherever possible. Guests keep coming back — which, in this business, is the only review that counts.

“Together we are able to combine the artistry of a commercially trained chef with the natural flair of a great home style cook. And we have lots of fun doing it.”

Ashley Parr
Chef

Ashley started as an apprentice chef at 15 and trained at TAFE SA. He worked at the Three Brothers Arms, The Magic Flute and the Lenzerheide Restaurant before becoming Head Chef of Dixon's Restaurant. He also makes the stove-top espresso the teachers write home about.

Kelly Parr
Cook

Kelly has ten years in hospitality, including five as assistant restaurant manager at the Goodwood Park Hotel. She auditioned and was shortlisted for MasterChef in 2009, and has a deep repertoire of dishes — scaled from a small retreat group up to a full camp.
